This is the central finding. Stage 2 scores a candidate community by summing attractiveness over the node's neighbours in it, with no normalisation for community size. A big community therefore always looks more attractive, and on dense graphs everything merges into one community. Concretely on the email-Eu graph: Stage 1 alone gives 335 communities with NMI 0.54; after Stage 2 there is one community and NMI is 0. The same collapse happens on LFR graphs at high noise. On sparse graphs like Cora the reverse happens: it stops with 214 communities when there are 7 true classes. The paper's benchmarks only used graphs where this does not show, which is why it went unnoticed.
